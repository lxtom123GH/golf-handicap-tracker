What do Par, CR and SR mean?

Par is the score a very good golfer is expected to shoot. CR (Course Rating) is what that golfer would actually average here — higher than par means a tough course. SR (Slope Rating) is how much harder the course gets for the rest of us (113 is average; higher = punishes mistakes more). They're printed on the scorecard and only affect your handicap maths — you don't need to do anything with them.

      New to handicaps? The 30-second version

      Every round you log gets a score differential — roughly "how well you played, adjusted for how hard the course was". Your Handicap Index is the average of your best differentials from your last 20 rounds (fewer while you're starting out). One bad round can't hurt it — only your best rounds count. It updates automatically every time you save a round here.

      Stableford points, the other number you'll see: 2 points for par on your handicap-adjusted score, 3 for birdie, 1 for bogey, 0 after that — so a blow-up hole stops costing you and you can pick up. Higher is better; 36 means you played to your handicap.

      Index vs Daily Handicap: your Handicap Index is your portable skill number — it follows you to any course. Your Daily Handicap is that number adjusted for the course and tees you're playing today (harder course → more strokes). Golf Australia's formula: Index × Slope ÷ 113 + (CR − Par), then × 0.93. On Course works it out for you the moment you pick a tee.

      What the strategies mean

      DECADE (Scott Fawcett): aim from your real dispersion — aggressive off the tee (usually driver), conservative into greens (centre-of-green bias, never short-side). Lowest Score Wins (Barzeski & Wedzik): map your "Shot Zone" (realistic dispersion) and pick targets where the whole zone finishes safe. Golf Sidekick 6-6-6 (break-80 method): ball in play off the tee, fat side of every green, no hero shots, take your medicine from trouble. Strokes-gained (Mark Broadie, "Every Shot Counts"): distance beats accuracy off the tee; most amateur approach misses are short, so take more club. No Flag Golf (Adam Young): the flag doesn't exist — centre of every green. Four Foundations (Jon Sherman, Practical Golf): "the war on double bogeys" — manage expectations, pick targets where your typical miss still leaves an easy next shot. Vision54 Think/Play Box (Nilsson & Marriott): decide everything behind the ball, then swing on pure feel — a routine discipline more than an aiming rule (your routine ✅/❌ taps measure exactly this). Elements of Scoring (Raymond Floyd): the classic — play within yourself, take what the course gives, never make the big number. Tiger-5 avoidance: forget birdies, just avoid the five card-wreckers — doubles, par-5 bogeys, three-putts, wedge-in-hand bogeys, blown easy saves. Personal par: play each hole to your par (a bogey on a hard par 4 is your par) — great while your handicap is high. Conservative control: 3-wood/hybrid off the tee, lay up to full-swing distances.

      While a period is active, the GPS widget shows that strategy's aim reminder on every shot — personalised with your own miss pattern once you've tapped 10+ results.
